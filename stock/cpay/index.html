What the numbers say

On the four Ms, Corpay earns 86 for management and 74 for moat. The management score rests on a return on invested capital of 8.8% and a gross margin of 69.9% — figures that describe a business turning capital into profit efficiently. The balance sheet carries debt at 2.60x equity, which magnifies both the returns above and the risk beneath them. A solid moat alongside exceptional management is a combination worth understanding before the price matters at all.

Three independent anchors set the price worth paying. Growth is modelled at 13.6% a year, below the 14.0% actually measured over the last decade — the model caps growth rather than extrapolating a good run forwards. The exit multiple assumed is 23.7x. That produces an intrinsic value of $346.10, a 10-CAP price of $256.47 and a payback time price of $380.72, with the value zone set at the highest of the three, $380.72. Today's price of $389.73 sits 2% above it.

A wonderful business at the wrong price is still the wrong price. Corpay scores well on the business and poorly on the entry point, which is the most common shape in a long bull market.
