What the numbers say

Carnival Corporation scores 39 for management and 63 for moat. The management score rests on a return on invested capital of 10.8% and a gross margin of 29.6% — figures that describe capital earning less than it costs to employ. The balance sheet carries debt at 2.28x equity, which magnifies both the returns above and the risk beneath them. The solid moat and weak management are not the same judgement, and the gap between them is where the argument about this business sits.

The price anchors are built on deliberately conservative assumptions. Growth is modelled at 15.0% a year, below the 37.0% actually measured over the last decade — the model caps growth rather than extrapolating a good run forwards. The exit multiple assumed is 16.9x. That produces an intrinsic value of $38.82, a 10-CAP price of $31.21 and a payback time price of $49.27, with the value zone set at the highest of the three, $49.27. Today's price of $25.07 sits inside that zone.

The price sits inside the value zone, but the business scores 63 for moat and 39 for management. Cheap and good are different tests, and only one of them is passed here.
