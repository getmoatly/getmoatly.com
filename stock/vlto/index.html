What the numbers say

Veralto scores 100 for management and 43 for moat. The management score rests on a return on invested capital of 16.1% and a gross margin of 59.9% — figures that describe a business turning capital into profit efficiently. Debt sits at 0.86x equity. A moderate moat alongside exceptional management is a combination worth understanding before the price matters at all.

The valuation runs on assumptions chosen to understate rather than flatter. Growth is modelled at 4.8% a year, below the 5.0% actually measured over the last decade — the model caps growth rather than extrapolating a good run forwards. The exit multiple assumed is 9.7x. That produces an intrinsic value of $15.13, a 10-CAP price of $43.28 and a payback time price of $43.05, with the value zone set at the highest of the three, $43.28. Today's price of $94.60 is 119% above it.

A wonderful business at the wrong price is still the wrong price. Veralto scores well on the business and poorly on the entry point, which is the most common shape in a long bull market.
