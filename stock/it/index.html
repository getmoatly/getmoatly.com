What the numbers say

Gartner scores 98 for management and 60 for moat. The management score rests on a return on invested capital of 18.8% and a gross margin of 67.7% — figures that describe a business turning capital into profit efficiently. The balance sheet carries debt at 11.31x equity, which magnifies both the returns above and the risk beneath them. A solid moat alongside exceptional management is a combination worth understanding before the price matters at all.

The price anchors are built on deliberately conservative assumptions. Growth is modelled at 9.6% a year, below the 10.0% actually measured over the last decade — the model caps growth rather than extrapolating a good run forwards. The exit multiple assumed is 19.3x. That produces an intrinsic value of $133.60, a 10-CAP price of $197.81 and a payback time price of $244.89, with the value zone set at the highest of the three, $244.89. Today's price of $192.80 sits inside that zone.

Gartner currently reads as a strong business at a price the model supports. That is rare enough to warrant asking what the market sees that these figures do not.
