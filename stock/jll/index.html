What the numbers say

Jones Lang LaSalle scores 68 for management and 81 for moat. The management score rests on a return on invested capital of 5.3% and a gross margin of 99.0% — figures that describe a business earning a respectable return on what it employs. Debt sits at 0.45x equity. The wide moat and strong management are not the same judgement, and the gap between them is where the argument about this business sits.

The price anchors are built on deliberately conservative assumptions. Growth is modelled at 9.5% a year, the rate measured from the filings. The exit multiple assumed is 19.0x. That produces an intrinsic value of $242.76, a 10-CAP price of $270.92 and a payback time price of $333.15, with the value zone set at the highest of the three, $333.15. Today's price of $308.07 sits inside that zone.

A business scoring this well, trading inside its value zone, is the combination the framework exists to find — which is also the reason to check why the market disagrees. A margin of safety score of 39 describes the cushion, not the certainty.
