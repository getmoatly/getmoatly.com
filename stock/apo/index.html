What the numbers say

Apollo Global Management scores 73 for management and 83 for moat. The management score rests on a return on invested capital of 5.2% and a gross margin of 95.5% — figures that describe a business earning a respectable return on what it employs. Debt sits at 0.57x equity. The wide moat and strong management are not the same judgement, and the gap between them is where the argument about this business sits.

Three independent anchors set the price worth paying. Growth is modelled at 15.0% a year, below the 69.0% actually measured over the last decade — the model caps growth rather than extrapolating a good run forwards. The exit multiple assumed is 25.9x. That produces an intrinsic value of $115.96, a 10-CAP price of $130.93 and a payback time price of $206.69, with the value zone set at the highest of the three, $206.69. Today's price of $114.34 sits inside that zone.

A business scoring this well, trading inside its value zone, is the combination the framework exists to find — which is also the reason to check why the market disagrees. A margin of safety score of 51 describes the cushion, not the certainty.
