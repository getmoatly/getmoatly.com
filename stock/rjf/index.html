What the numbers say

On the four Ms, Raymond James Financial earns 82 for management and 75 for moat. The management score rests on a return on invested capital of 15.9% and a gross margin of 88.2% — figures that describe a business turning capital into profit efficiently. Debt sits at 0.36x equity. The solid moat and exceptional management are not the same judgement, and the gap between them is where the argument about this business sits.

The price anchors are built on deliberately conservative assumptions. Growth is modelled at 14.6% a year, below the 15.0% actually measured over the last decade — the model caps growth rather than extrapolating a good run forwards. The exit multiple assumed is 16.4x. That produces an intrinsic value of $180.97, a 10-CAP price of $118.21 and a payback time price of $182.85, with the value zone set at the highest of the three, $182.85. Today's price of $159.76 sits inside that zone.

A business scoring this well, trading inside its value zone, is the combination the framework exists to find — which is also the reason to check why the market disagrees. A margin of safety score of 57 describes the cushion, not the certainty.
