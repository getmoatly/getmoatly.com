What the numbers say

On the four Ms, Wells Fargo & Company earns 31 for management and 42 for moat. The management score rests on a return on invested capital of 3.2% and a gross margin of 64.8% — figures that describe capital earning less than it costs to employ. The balance sheet carries debt at 2.35x equity, which magnifies both the returns above and the risk beneath them. The moderate moat and weak management are not the same judgement, and the gap between them is where the argument about this business sits.

The price anchors are built on deliberately conservative assumptions. Growth is modelled at 8.5% a year, the rate measured from the filings. The exit multiple assumed is 14.6x. That produces an intrinsic value of $56.39, a 10-CAP price of $62.24 and a payback time price of $73.07, with the value zone set at the highest of the three, $73.07. Today's price of $80.27 sits 10% above it.

The price is above the value zone and the business scores 42 for moat and 31 for management. Neither test argues for paying up here.
