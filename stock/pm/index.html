What the numbers say

Philip Morris International scores 74 for management and 47 for moat. The management score rests on a return on invested capital of 25.6% and a gross margin of 67.1% — figures that describe a business earning a respectable return on what it employs. Shareholder equity is negative, so the debt-to-equity figure is not meaningful here — usually the mark of heavy buybacks rather than distress, but worth checking. A moderate moat alongside strong management is a combination worth understanding before the price matters at all.

The valuation runs on assumptions chosen to understate rather than flatter. Growth is modelled at 7.2% a year, the rate measured from the filings. The exit multiple assumed is 14.4x. That produces an intrinsic value of $49.81, a 10-CAP price of $84.36 and a payback time price of $93.52, with the value zone set at the highest of the three, $93.52. Today's price of $188.18 is 101% above it.
