What the numbers say

On the four Ms, TransDigm Group earns 36 for management and 70 for moat. The management score rests on a return on invested capital of 15.2% and a gross margin of 60.1% — figures that describe capital earning less than it costs to employ. Shareholder equity is negative, so the debt-to-equity figure is not meaningful here — usually the mark of heavy buybacks rather than distress, but worth checking. A solid moat alongside weak management is a combination worth understanding before the price matters at all.

Three independent anchors set the price worth paying. Growth is modelled at 11.6% a year, below the 12.0% actually measured over the last decade — the model caps growth rather than extrapolating a good run forwards. The exit multiple assumed is 23.2x. That produces an intrinsic value of $565.72, a 10-CAP price of $346.86 and a payback time price of $469.07, with the value zone set at the highest of the three, $469.07. Today's price of $1,097.24 is 134% above it.

The price is above the value zone and the business scores 70 for moat and 36 for management. Neither test argues for paying up here.
